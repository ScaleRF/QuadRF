ARCHITECTURE & SIGNAL CHAIN

IEEE 802.11a OFDM PHY & C-BAND FRONTEND

802.11 OFDM Link implements a standards-structured IEEE 802.11a OFDM transceiver bridging a Linux tun0 IPv4 network interface across two QuadRF phased-array stations in the 4.9–6.0 GHz C-band. Each 20 MHz OFDM symbol uses a 64-point FFT (312.5 kHz subcarrier spacing, 3.2 µs IFFT period + 0.8 µs cyclic prefix = 4.0 µs symbol duration) with 48 data subcarriers, 4 BPSK pilot subcarriers (k = ±7, ±21), a nulled DC subcarrier, and 11 virtual guard subcarriers.

40 MS/s HALF-BAND RESAMPLING & DAC/ADC SCALING

To prevent 20 MS/s zero-order-hold spectral images at ±20 MHz from folding through the wideband 40 MHz MAX2850/MAX2851 analog baseband filters, both TX (MIPI DSI) and RX (MIPI CSI-2) streams run at 40 MS/s. On transmit, the 20 MS/s complex OFDM waveform is upsampled 2× through a 15-tap anti-imaging half-band FIR filter, smoothly raised-cosine windowed over 12 samples at burst edges, and normalized to a peak digital DAC amplitude of 96 counts (scaled by 1/√Ntx when multiple TX elements are active). On receive, the 40 MS/s CSI-2 stream is filtered and decimated 2× by a matched 15-tap half-band FIR filter to 20 MS/s, suppressing out-of-band noise by 3 dB and rejecting alias images by >45 dB.

PPDU FRAME STRUCTURE (PREAMBLE, SIGNAL & DATA)

Every transmitted frame begins with a 16 µs PLCP preamble: 10 Short Training Symbols (STS, 10 × 16 = 160 samples) for packet detection, AGC evaluation, and coarse CFO estimation, followed by a 32-sample cyclic prefix and two 64-sample Long Training Symbols (LTF1, LTF2, 160 samples total) for fine symbol timing, fine CFO estimation, and per-subcarrier channel estimation.

The SIGNAL symbol (1 OFDM symbol, always BPSK r = 1/2) encodes the payload rate (6, 9, 12, 18, 24, 36, 48, or 54 Mb/s), 12-bit MPDU byte length, and even parity bit. Data symbols are scrambled by the 802.11 LFSR (S(x) = x7 + x4 + 1), encoded with the K = 7 industry-standard convolutional code (g0 = 1338, g1 = 1718) with rate-dependent puncturing (1/2, 2/3, 3/4), block interleaved, and Gray-mapped to BPSK, QPSK, 16-QAM, or 64-QAM.

VIRTUAL-CARRIER CIR DENOISING & PILOT CPE/SRO TRACKING

At the receiver, the raw LTF channel estimate Hraw[k] is computed on the 52 active subcarriers and linearly interpolated across the DC null (k = 0) before transforming to the 64-tap time-domain channel impulse response (CIR). Retaining the 16-tap multipath support (12 causal + 4 anti-causal taps) and zeroing the remaining 48 noise-only taps removes out-of-window thermal noise and eliminates artificial sinc ringing at band edges k = ±26.

For each data symbol n, the 4 pilot subcarriers (k = ±7, ±21) are equalized and compared against the polarity-scrambled reference pilots to extract both the Common Phase Error (CPE) and the linear phase slope across frequency caused by residual sampling rate and timing offset (SRO). Soft log-likelihood ratios (LLRs) are weighted by per-subcarrier channel power |H[k]|2 before soft-decision Viterbi decoding and IEEE 802.11 32-bit FCS verification.

MIPI DSI VSYNC TURNAROUND, ARQ & TUN0 IP BRIDGE

Because the RP1 MIPI DSI transmitter scans out staging buffers on 19.34 ms DRM vertical sync (VSYNC) boundaries, each TX burst arms the SE5003L 1W PA strictly around the active frame scanout and immediately flips back to an all-zero staging buffer on the next VSYNC edge. A 40 ms TDD turnaround guard combined with real-time carrier-sense energy detection prevents half-duplex collisions. Link-layer Stop-and-Wait ARQ with standalone and piggybacked ACKs, asymmetric backoff, and automatic TX queue packet coalescing provides lossless delivery for both ICMP/UDP/TCP traffic on tun0 (10.99.0.x/24) and live station chat.
